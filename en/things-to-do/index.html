More than a place to sleep. Stay at Meihodo, and step into Japanese culture.

Surrounded by the nature of Aso, Meihodo offers far more than a room and a meal. Kyūdō, tameshigiri, tea ceremony, bonseki, taiko drumming — a whole range of Japanese cultural experiences waits on the estate itself. Several of them are held indoors, in the tea house, the great hall and the martial arts hall, so a rainy day is still easy to plan around.

Japanese culture, inside the place you're staying.

Meihodo is a cultural estate of 37 buildings spread across 56,000 square metres. A martial arts hall, an archery range, a tea house, a taiko hall, a great hall — the buildings where Japanese culture is practised are all on the grounds, so guests can join an experience between other parts of the day without travelling anywhere.

Rather than moving from one sight to the next, you settle in one place: you touch Japanese culture, learn a little of it, and try it with your own hands. An evening after check-in, a morning before you set off — the day is yours to shape.

Rain in Aso? Your day at Meihodo still works.

You planned the trip for months, and it rains from breakfast onward. In Aso, that happens.

Several of the cultural experiences at Meihodo are held indoors: tea ceremony in the tea house (Seirensha), bonseki in the great hall, kendō and karate in the martial arts hall. When the outdoor sightseeing is off, the day is still straightforward to put together.

  • Tea ceremony is held in the tea house (Seirensha), bonseki in the great hall, and kendō and karate in the martial arts hall — all indoors.
  • For kyūdō, taiko, ikebana and tameshigiri the venue can change with group size and the weather on the day; please ask when you book.
  • Dinner is served at Ryotei Meihodo on the estate, so the day needs no travelling.
  • Takigyo is held outdoors and may be cancelled in bad weather.

If you are looking for indoor things to do in Aso on a wet day, the cultural experiences here are worth considering. Weather often reshuffles a Kumamoto itinerary; keeping an indoor session in reserve means the day still works.

Aso is famous for its landscape, and rightly so. Pairing the outdoors with an indoor session means a rainy morning usually costs nothing more than a change of order.

A day at Meihodo

One example. Experience times can be arranged when you book.

  1. 15:00

    Check in

    Arrive at your building, surrounded by the nature of Aso, and take a moment to settle in.

  2. 16:00

    A cultural experience

    Kyūdō, tea ceremony, taiko — whichever draws you. Most sessions run about an hour.

  3. 18:00

    Dinner

    Seasonal Japanese cuisine at Ryotei Meihodo, a short walk across the estate.

  4. Evening

    A quiet Aso night

    With the lights low the grounds are remarkably still. Stay in your room or wander the estate.

  5. Next morning

    Breakfast and a walk

    After breakfast, stroll the grounds and head out into Aso. Check-out is at 11:00.
